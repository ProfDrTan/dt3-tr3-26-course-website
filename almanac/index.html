The Almanac Agent — How to Use It

What is the Stock Trader's Almanac?

Published every year since 1968, it's a data book of historical stock market patterns — what the market has done in each month, each week, and around specific dates over the past 50–75 years. It does not predict the future — it shows you what has historically happened and how often. Your job as the Almanac agent is to read the relevant patterns for the week and turn them into a structured hypothesis, not a certainty. Prof Tan will provide the current Almanac pages for your team to work from — this page teaches you how to actually read them.

2026 Context You Need First

2026 is a midterm election year — this changes how you read every pattern.

Before reading any monthly page, understand the 4-Year Presidential Cycle — the most important standing context in the whole Almanac this year.

Q2–Q3 2026 — The Weak Spot

The Almanac calls Q2–Q3 of every midterm year the "Weak Spot." Historical averages across these two quarters: Dow −2.0%, S&P −2.5%, Nasdaq −6.6%. Sprint 2 (late September) sits inside this window.

Bottom Risk

10 of the last 16 bear markets bottomed in a midterm year. Doesn't mean this year will — it means the historical base rate for volatility is elevated right now, not a normal year to read patterns against.

Q4 2026 — Sweet Spot Ahead

After midterm weakness, Q4 historically begins the "Sweet Spot" — the best period of the 4-year cycle. Worth knowing where the cycle is heading, even while reading a weaker current quarter.

How this affects your output: when you read any bullish weekly pattern, weigh it against this Q2–Q3 headwind. A bullish week-level pattern means less in a midterm year than it would in a stronger part of the cycle. Always state both the specific pattern and the cycle context — never one without the other.

Step-by-Step: How to Write Your Almanac Output

Step 1 — Identify the relevant pages

Each week, look up three things in whatever Almanac pages Prof Tan provides: (1) the Monthly Almanac page for the current month, (2) day-specific notes for the coming week, (3) the Sector Seasonality table. You don't need to read the whole book — just these targeted lookups.

Step 2 — Record the four key facts
Fact to recordWhere to find it
Monthly rank and average % changeMonthly Vital Statistics table, top rows
Up/Down record for the monthSame table, Up/Down row
Specific week/day patternDaily calendar page notes for that week
Relevant sector seasonalitiesSector Seasonality table
Step 3 — Assess pattern strength

Not all patterns are equal. Use this framework every time:

STRONG
Occurs 70%+ of the time across 20+ years.
MODERATE
Occurs 55–70% of the time. Useful context, not dominant.
WEAK — use with caution
Under 55%, or recent years conflict with the historical record.
Step 4 — Write the structured output

This is what goes into your team's report. Use this exact structure every week — fill in real figures once Prof Tan provides the current pages.

Almanac Agent Output Template MONTH: <current month> 2026
CYCLE CONTEXT: Midterm election year. Q2–Q3 is the "Weak Spot" of the 4-Year Cycle.

MONTHLY STATS:
 - Rank, Up/Down record, average % change per index
 - Midterm-year-specific average (if the Almanac lists it separately)

SPECIFIC WEEK PATTERN:
 - Named pattern, historical record, pattern strength (Strong/Moderate/Weak)
 - Note if multiple patterns contradict each other

SECTOR SIGNALS:
 - Which sectors are in a seasonal Long or Short window right now
 - Flag any contradiction between the seasonal pattern and actual current price action

ALMANAC SEASONAL BIAS: <bullish / bearish / neutral>, one sentence why

PATTERN CONFIDENCE: LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH

ALMANAC THESIS: One paragraph combining all of the above into a single hypothesis with a stated confidence level

Source: Stock Trader's Almanac 2026, page numbers cited
The most important rule for the Almanac Agent: Never write "the Almanac says the market will go up this week." Always write "the seasonal pattern suggests X, with Y confidence, because Z — and this conflicts/aligns with the macro/technical evidence as follows." The Almanac gives you probability context, not certainty. Your job is translating a historical pattern into a structured hypothesis with an honest confidence level.
