TOOL REFERENCE
Fed Funds Futures & the CME FedWatch Tool

Before your next macro entry, know this tool — it's the single fastest way to know what the market itself expects the Fed to do, not a guess or a pundit's opinion.

What it is: the CME FedWatch Tool derives real-time, market-implied probabilities for upcoming Fed rate decisions from 30-Day Fed Funds futures prices — contracts traders actually buy and sell based on where they think rates are headed.

How the math works, roughly: take a futures contract's price and subtract it from 100 — the result is the rate the market is implying for that month. Compare that against the Fed's current target range, and you get a probability split across hike / hold / cut for the next FOMC meeting.

Why it's market-moving: this isn't a lagging indicator — it's the market pricing in the future *before* it happens. When FedWatch's probabilities shift sharply (say, from 60% hold to 80% hike), that shift itself often moves stocks, bonds, and the dollar immediately, well before the actual Fed decision. Tracking FedWatch alongside a headline event — like this week's yield/FOMC story above — tells you whether the market's expectations are stable or actively repricing.

For your R2 reports: check FedWatch's current probability split before writing your FED_IMPLICATION field — "90% hike odds" is a much stronger, more specific claim than "the Fed may hike."

HOW TO READ IT — WORKED EXAMPLE
Interpreting the FedWatch Screen, Using Today's Real Numbers

First time looking at this tool, it's a lot on one screen. Here's what each part actually means, using the live 16 Sep 2026 meeting data.

1. The date tabs across the top — each one is a future FOMC meeting. You almost always want "Current," the nearest one — that's tomorrow's meeting in our case.

2. The bar chart — each bar is a possible outcome, with its probability. Today's real chart shows two bars: 350–375 bps at 7.7% (the Fed holds steady) and 375–400 bps at 92.3% (the Fed hikes). Read this as: the market thinks a hike is nearly certain.

3. The EASE / NO CHANGE / HIKE columns — same information as the bars, just as numbers. Ease = cut, No Change = hold, Hike = raise. Today: 0.0% / 7.7% / 92.3%.

4. The bottom table — this is the part most people skip, and it's the most important one. It shows the same probability at four points in time: Now, 1 Day Ago, 1 Week Ago, 1 Month Ago. Today's real numbers for "No Change" (holding steady):

Now1 Day Ago1 Week Ago1 Month Ago
7.7%6.5%40.6%66.9%

Why this matters more than the current number alone: a month ago, the market thought a hold was more likely than not (66.9%). One week ago, that had already fallen to 40.6%. Now it's just 7.7%. That's not a static forecast — that's a genuine sentiment collapse, and it happened over about three weeks. That shift is what should have been feeding your Almanac/Macro reports all along, not just today's snapshot.

What to actually watch, going forward: don't just screenshot today's bars. Check back after tomorrow's decision and see whether the outcome matched the 92.3% — and next time you check FedWatch for any future meeting, look at the trend columns first. A probability that's been stable for a month tells a different story than one that just flipped in the last week.
