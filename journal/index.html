TOOL REFERENCE
Fed Funds Futures & the CME FedWatch Tool

Before your next macro entry, know this tool — it's the single fastest way to know what the market itself expects the Fed to do, not a guess or a pundit's opinion.

What it is: the CME FedWatch Tool derives real-time, market-implied probabilities for upcoming Fed rate decisions from 30-Day Fed Funds futures prices — contracts traders actually buy and sell based on where they think rates are headed.

How the math works, roughly: take a futures contract's price and subtract it from 100 — the result is the rate the market is implying for that month. Compare that against the Fed's current target range, and you get a probability split across hike / hold / cut for the next FOMC meeting.

Why it's market-moving: this isn't a lagging indicator — it's the market pricing in the future *before* it happens. When FedWatch's probabilities shift sharply (say, from 60% hold to 80% hike), that shift itself often moves stocks, bonds, and the dollar immediately, well before the actual Fed decision. Tracking FedWatch alongside a headline event — like this week's yield/FOMC story above — tells you whether the market's expectations are stable or actively repricing.

For your R2 reports: check FedWatch's current probability split before writing your FED_IMPLICATION field — "90% hike odds" is a much stronger, more specific claim than "the Fed may hike."
