Why This, Why Now

If you don't know what SPX is, what sector rotation means, or what an Almanac agent does — good. That's the starting point this course is built for, not a gap you're expected to have already closed.

The honest pushback, addressed directly: "I'm an IT student. Why do I need to know finance?" You don't need to become a trader. You need enough literacy that nobody can take advantage of you with money for the rest of your life — and this course is the cheapest, lowest-stakes place to build that, with a real project instead of a textbook.

Three Reasons This Is Worth Your Time

1

Compounding rewards starting early — and you're at the one age it works best

The single biggest variable in long-term compounding isn't skill or luck — it's time. Being in your early 20s is an advantage that literally cannot be bought back later. You don't need to be good at this yet. You need to start being curious about it now, while the years ahead of you are still long.

2

Financial literacy is self-defense, not a career pivot

You don't need to trade for a living. You need to understand enough — what a market move means, how to read a macro headline, what compounding actually does over decades — that decisions about your own money are yours, made with real understanding, not guesswork or someone else's sales pitch.

3

The real subject of this trimester is Agile and Scrum — this is the vehicle, not the destination

Market intelligence is the project. Sprints, standups, retrospectives, working as a real team under real deadlines — that's what you're actually being trained in, and it transfers to literally any domain you work in after this. The discomfort of not knowing what SPX means yet is the same discomfort as not knowing what Git meant the first time you saw it. It resolves the same way: by using it.

What This Looks Like in Practice

You don't need to arrive knowing any of this. Sprint 2 has you gather a handful of real market facts by hand — not because the facts themselves are the point, but because you can't do it without looking up what they mean. You'll learn the vocabulary by needing it, not by being lectured at first.
