How to Divide the Six Roles

Six roles, but teams aren't all the same size. Find your team size below.

8 Members
One person each on R1, R3, R4, R5. Two people together on R2 (Almanac & Macro) and two together on R6 (LLM & GitHub) — those two carry the heaviest weekly workload, so splitting them across a pair makes sense.
7 Members
One person each on R1, R2, R3, R4, R5. Two people together on R6 (LLM & GitHub) — the role with the most moving parts each week.
6 Members
One person, one role. No doubling up needed.
5 Members
One person covers R1 and R4 together (Product Owner & Scrum Master, plus Data & Calibration) — both are tracking and process roles, so it's a natural pairing. The other four roles (R2, R3, R5, R6) each get their own person.
All Six Roles — Responsibilities

Every Role Has a Weekend Job and a Monday Speaking Point

R1 — Product Owner & Scrum Master
Sprint Goal Guardian + Process Flow Protector
Weekend: Confirm this sprint's goal and validate the Definition of Done. Coordinate deadlines, remove blockers, ensure every role participates, run a brief retro check. Make the final call on which prediction represents the team's best work.

Monday: State the sprint goal, DoD status, and deliver the final prediction with confidence level. Present the retrospective: what failed, what improved, one specific process change.
R2 — Almanac & Macro Analyst
Historical Pattern + Macro-Economic Analyst
Weekend: Pull seasonal patterns, historical analogues, and calendar signals (Fed meetings, options expiry, earnings season). Analyse the macro environment: inflation, Fed language, bond yields, dollar strength, sector rotation. Produce written output before LLMs are queried.

Monday: Present the key seasonal insight and the macro thesis supporting or undermining the prediction.
R3 — Technical Analyst
Chart & Pattern Analyst
Weekend: Analyse SPX, NDX, IWM charts. Identify key levels, trend structure, momentum signals, and volume patterns. Write independent technical output before LLMs.

Monday: Present chart evidence and explain why the technical picture supports (or contradicts) the prediction.
R4 — Data & Calibration Lead
Evidence, Accuracy & Learning Tracker
Weekend: Record actual outcomes against predictions and calculate accuracy. Maintain the calibration suite, tracking directional accuracy per model and per agent over time. Identify systematic biases and feed findings into R5's Human Score input.

Monday: Present the outcome vs. prediction, the calibration trend, and which model has the best track record so far.
R5 — Human Score Lead ⭐ Most Important
Wild Card & Override Lead
Weekend: After all agents and LLMs have reported, review the synthesis. Apply the Human Score. Decide whether to confirm, adjust, or override the AI consensus. Justify the Wild Card observation.

Monday: Present the Human Score reasoning. This is the primary marks differentiator. Weak R5 = weak team score.
R6 — LLM & GitHub Operator
Multi-Model Comparison + Evidence & Release Manager
Weekend: Query the three LLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini, DeepSeek) with identical prompts — Claude optional if you have access. Build a comparison table. Commit all evidence files, keep the folder structure clean, create the correct release tag for the sprint, verify the release link is accessible before the deadline.

Monday: Present model agreement, divergence, and what that reveals about uncertainty. Open GitHub live, show the release tag, walk through the evidence folder.
