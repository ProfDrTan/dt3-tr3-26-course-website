What each check means in Scrum

  • Sprint Backlog is visible (FigJam). Scrum depends on transparency. Your team's section of the shared board shows this sprint's work as cards, each with its Definition of Done, moving from To Do to Done.
  • Daily Scrum is happening (Discord). The team inspects progress and adapts every day, in your team channel, the course's only official channel. Fewer than 10 messages in 48 hours fails this check.
  • Increment is on main (GitHub). Each sprint produces a working increment. Only work merged into main counts, and no commits for three days means the team has stalled.
  • Sprint timebox kept (GitHub). A sprint has fixed deadlines. A prediction committed after its lock time, or a part not on main by its due date, fails this check.
  • Whole team involved (GitHub and Discord). A Scrum team is self-organising, and everyone contributes. At least half the team should be active each week. This is counted for the team only; no names are ever shown.

Following Scrum: no check failed. Drifting from Scrum: one check failed. Not following Scrum: two or more checks failed, a prediction lock was missed, or there have been no GitHub commits for three days or more.

How the board is checked, and why there are no appeals

  • Only three places count: your team's main branch on GitHub, your team's section of the shared FigJam board, and your team's Discord channel. Work kept anywhere else (another branch, a copied board, WhatsApp) can't be seen, so it doesn't count. These are rules you already work under.
  • Every update is a snapshot with the time it was taken, shown at the top. Anything you change after that time appears in the next update, not this one.
  • Every result links to its evidence. The GitHub link opens the exact commit that was the latest one when the check was made, so later commits can't change what it shows. The FigJam link opens your team's section of the board. GitHub links only open for members of that team (the repos are private); anyone else sees a "not found" page, and that is expected.
