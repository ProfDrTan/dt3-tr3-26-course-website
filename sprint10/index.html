Sprint 10: Two weeks of evidence

Sprint 10 runs from Tue 17 Nov to Demo Day, Mon 23 Nov. It is the last sprint of the trimester.

Sprint 10 goal: by Demo Day, every team can show, with its own locked numbers, how its prediction system performed over two A/B weeks (vW10 and vW11), and which method it would keep.

The idea in one minute

In Sprint 9B you tested your change on one week and gave each market a verdict: keep, adjust or drop. One week can be luck. Sprint 10 runs the same test on a second week, so your Demo Day result rests on two weeks, not one.

What it isFile
A · Old methodYour prediction made the way you made vW9, as if nothing had changed.predictions/vW11_old.json
B · New methodThe same prediction with your Sprint 9B verdict applied (see milestone 2).predictions/vW11.json
Baseline · Do nothingTuesday 17 Nov’s close, the last close anyone knows at the Wednesday lock. Your script adds it; you do not lock it.added by engine/check_error.py

All three face the same market on Friday 20 Nov. Same test, same rules, same files as Sprint 9B, one week later.

No locked vW10 in Sprint 9B? Then vW11 is your first locked A/B test. Use the rules in your docs/change.md as written, lock both vW11 files on time, and on Demo Day say plainly that you have one locked week, not two. One honest locked week is worth far more than two unlocked ones.

1. Sprint 10 backlog (FigJam + GitHub)

One card for each of milestones 2–5 and one for the Demo Day presentation, each with an owner and a one-line “done” statement, in your team’s FigJam section. Copy the same cards into docs/backlog-10.md and merge it into main by the deadline. FigJam is where you work; the GitHub copy is what records the time.

2. Rules for vW11

In docs/change-10.md, one section per market, written by that market’s owner. Each section applies the verdict from your docs/did_it_work.md, and nothing else:

  • Keep: copy the Sprint 9B rule unchanged.
  • Adjust: write the rule with the one adjustment your verdict described, and quote that sentence from did_it_work.md.
  • Drop: write Rule: no change. The new-method number for that market is then the same as the old-method number.
docs/change-10.md
## SPX
Verdict from did_it_work.md: adjust ("halve the correction")
Rule: New-method SPX = old-method SPX reduced by half our median SPX error
over vW4–vW10 (in %).
Uses only: Tuesday 17 Nov close, data/errors.csv (vW4–vW10).

No brand-new rules this week. A rule that changes every week cannot be tested, and the point of Sprint 10 is a second, comparable result. Each rule must use only information available before Wed 18 Nov 23:59 SGT (the latest US close then is Tuesday 17 Nov) and must produce a number.

3. Two vW11 locks

Exactly the Sprint 9B format: numbers only, no % signs, no actual closes, both locked by the deadline, and the old-method file must not borrow anything from the new rule.

Typo check before you lock (R1). Compare every number in both files with Tuesday 17 Nov’s close. Anything more than 10% away is almost certainly a typo. Fix it before 23:59: after the lock it stays as it is. Your sealed files vW4.json to vW10.json and vW10_old.json stay exactly as they are.

4. Two-week errors and chart

Run engine/check_error.py after the Friday close so that data/errors.csv gets three vW11 rows per market, in the same format as vW10:

week,target_date,instrument,method,predicted,actual,error,unit
vW11,2026-11-20,SPX,old,7600,[Fri close],[..],pct
vW11,2026-11-20,SPX,new,7560,[Fri close],[..],pct
vW11,2026-11-20,SPX,baseline,[Tue close],[Fri close],[..],pct

Same rules as Sprint 9B: actual closes from Yahoo Finance (^GSPC, ^NDX, GCZ26.CMX, ^TNX); error in % for SPX, NDX and GOLD, in basis points for TNX. The US market closes on Friday 20 Nov at 16:00 New York time, about 05:00 SGT on Saturday 21 Nov. Run the script after that and check that the vW11 actual dates say 20 Nov. Do not change your vW4–vW10 rows.

On ui/index.html, extend each market’s A/B chart to show both weeks: old, new and baseline for vW10 and for vW11, with a zero line.

5. Final verdict

In docs/final_verdict.md, one short paragraph per market, written by that market’s owner. Each paragraph must:

  • quote the vW10 and vW11 error rows from data/errors.csv (six rows; three if you have only vW11);
  • give the average size of the error (ignore the sign) for old, new and baseline across the locked weeks, and say which was smallest;
  • give a final verdict: which method your system would use from now on, and one sentence on what would make you change your mind.

Two weeks is still not proof, and saying so is part of a good answer. A team that shows clearly that its change did not beat the baseline has done the job well.
