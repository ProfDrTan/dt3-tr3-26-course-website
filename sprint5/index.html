How to read the close

The close is the close of Friday's New York session, which lands early Saturday morning in Singapore. Check Saturday morning or later.

  1. Open the market's Historical Data page on Yahoo Finance, set to Daily.
  2. Find the row for the date.
  3. Read the Close column, not Adj Close.

Gold: use GCZ26.CMX for both Fridays. An identical close counts as no result.

How to read the 10-year yield (^TNX)

What it is: ^TNX is Cboe's index of the yield on the US 10-year Treasury note, the interest rate the US government pays to borrow for ten years. You are predicting that yield, a percentage, not the price of a bond and not a futures contract. Yields and bond prices move in opposite directions: when bonds sell off, the yield goes up.

  1. Open Yahoo Finance · ^TNX history. The page title must read CBOE Interest Rate 10 Year T No (^TNX).
  2. Set it to Daily and find the row for Friday 9 Oct.
  3. Read the Close column. Yahoo shows four decimals, for example 4.6640. Round to two decimals (4.66), which means 4.66%.
  4. Do the same for Friday 2 Oct, so you know the starting point your call moves from.

When the close lands: Yahoo stamps the ^TNX close at about 2:00 pm Chicago time, which is about 03:00 Saturday in Singapore. Check Saturday morning or later. Don't use the live number you see during Friday's session.

Five traps that will give you the wrong number:

  • Ten times too big. Some sites show the same index multiplied by ten: 46.64 instead of 4.66. If your number is above 20, you're on the wrong scale.
  • A different series. FRED's 10-year series (DGS10) and the US Treasury's own daily rate are calculated differently and published a day later, so they can differ by a few basis points. Don't use them for your call or your actual.
  • A different feed. CNBC's US10Y and broker screens use other price sources. Use Yahoo ^TNX only, for both your call and the actual, so every team is scored against the same number.
  • A futures price. Ten-year Treasury futures (ZN) trade around 110, which is a price, not a yield. Not what you're predicting.
  • The wrong direction. "Bonds rally" means yields fall. Read the sentence twice before you turn news into a call.

How it's scored: the miss in basis points. One basis point is 0.01 percentage points, so 4.27 versus 4.31 is a 4 basis-point miss.

Example (invented numbers): Friday 2 Oct close 4.27. R6 calls 4.31 because a stronger-than-expected inflation release is the week's main catalyst. Friday 9 Oct close 4.35. The miss is 4 basis points, and the direction (up) was right.
